## What it is

Security is about keeping what's yours from being taken: your accounts, your
device, your money, your name. It's the world of locks and keys — passcodes,
two-factor codes, the switch that lets you erase a stolen phone from your
laptop. When security works, the wrong people stay out.

Privacy is a different question: who gets to know what about you. A service
can answer the security question perfectly and the privacy question terribly.

Think of a bank. The vault is genuinely secure — thick steel, cameras, alarms.
And the bank still sees every transaction you make. Nobody robbed you.
You have no privacy from the bank. Both things are true at once, because
security and privacy are different properties.

## Why it's a problem

The words get used interchangeably, and that mix-up costs people in both
directions.

In one direction: "the app is secure, so I'm fine." Plenty of apps with
excellent encryption and spotless security records make their money by
recording what you do and selling access to the pattern. The padlock icon in
your browser means your connection can't be read in transit. It says nothing
about what the company on the other end does with what you send — and the
padlock shows up just as proudly on the surveillance economy's front doors.

In the other direction: privacy without security collapses. You can be
careful about what you share, but if your email account has a reused password
and no second factor, one breach hands someone your inbox — and your inbox
can reset nearly everything else. All the discretion in the world doesn't
survive an account takeover.

The point isn't to pick a favorite. They fail separately, so they have to be
handled separately.

## What you can do

Run two checklists, not one.

**The security checklist** protects against taking: a passcode worth the name,
two-factor authentication on the accounts that matter (email first — it's the
skeleton key), software updates applied, and a way to find or erase a lost
device before it becomes someone else's project.

**The privacy checklist** protects against watching: app permissions cut back
to what each app plausibly needs, ad tracking turned off, location access
downgraded from "Always" wherever you find it.
