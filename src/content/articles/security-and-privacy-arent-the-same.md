## What it is

Security is about keeping what's yours from being taken: your accounts, your
device, your money, your name. It's the world of locks and keys — passcodes,
two-factor codes, the switch that lets you erase a stolen phone from your
laptop. When security works, the wrong people stay out.

Privacy is a different question: who gets to know what about you. A service
can answer the security question perfectly and the privacy question terribly.

Think of a bank. The vault is genuinely secure — thick steel, cameras, alarms.
And the bank still sees every transaction you make. Nobody robbed you.
You have no privacy from the bank. Both things are true at once, because
security and privacy are different properties.

## Why it's a problem

The words get used interchangeably, and the mix-up breaks people's defenses
in both directions.

In one direction: "the app is secure, so I'm fine." Plenty of apps with
excellent encryption make their money by recording what you do and selling
access to the pattern. The padlock icon in your browser means your connection
can't be read in transit. It says nothing about what the company on the other
end does with what you send — and the padlock shows up just as proudly on the
surveillance economy's front doors.

In the other direction: privacy without security collapses. You can be
careful about what you share, but if your email account has a reused password
and no second factor, one breach at some site you forgot about hands someone
your inbox. They fail separately — which means they have to be defended
separately.

## What it costs

When the security side fails, it fails like dominoes. Your inbox can reset
nearly every other account you own, so one takeover becomes many: drained
balances, hijacked social accounts messaging your friends with the next scam,
and days or weeks of resets, support calls, and proving you're you to get it
all back. A lost phone with a weak passcode is the same cascade in pocket
form — whoever holds it holds your email, your photos, your saved cards.

When the privacy side fails, the bill arrives more quietly. The "secure" app
that watched everything built a profile that gets sold, merged, and
eventually breached — and unlike a password, the facts of your life can't be
rotated. You can reset a credential. You can't reset where you've been.
