## What you'll be able to do afterward

- An unexpected call about money or accounts triggers your check, not your panic.
- A cloned voice or spoofed caller ID can't rush you into anything.
- Nobody gets a login code out of you — ever.
- The most common pressure tactics read as the giveaways they are.

## The plays

<ol class="plays">
<li>
<strong>Hang up and call back on a number you already trust</strong>
<span class="path">When: "your bank," "your card company," or any account provider contacts you first</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: real institutions survive the call-back check every time. Impersonators lose the thread the moment you dial the number on the back of your card instead.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Treat urgency as a stop sign</strong>
<span class="path">When: anything must happen "right now" — fees, arrests, expiring offers, stranded relatives</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: urgency exists to keep you from verifying. Legitimate problems survive a five-minute pause; scams almost never do.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Never read out or type a code you didn't request</strong>
<span class="path">When: anyone asks for a texted or emailed code "to verify you"</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: that code is the final step of a login — theirs, into your account. No real company calls you and asks you to read one back.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Set a family code word</strong>
<span class="path">With: anyone who might ever call you needing emergency money</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: a cloned voice can sound exactly like family. A code word turns "sounds like" back into proof, and it costs nothing.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Let unknown callers go to voicemail</strong>
<span class="path">Where: your phone's silence-unknown-callers setting — or simply don't pick up</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: scam scripts need a live person to pressure. Voicemail strips the urgency and gives you time to check the claim on your terms.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Judge the payment method, not the story</strong>
<span class="path">When: anyone directs you to gift cards, wire transfers, crypto ATMs, or payment apps for an "official" debt</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: no government agency or legitimate business collects money in gift cards. The payment channel is the confession, no matter how good the story was.</p>
</li>
</ol>

## You're now covered against…

- **Impersonation calls and texts** — the call-back habit beats spoofed caller ID and perfect-looking messages.
- **Voice-clone emergencies** — the code word works no matter how convincing the audio is.
- **Code-theft takeovers** — your two-factor codes only ever go one direction: into a login screen you opened.
- **Pressure tactics** — urgency and odd payment methods now read as evidence, not motivation.

Ten minutes, and most of it is deciding once instead of deciding under
pressure. That's the whole trick — theirs, and now yours.
