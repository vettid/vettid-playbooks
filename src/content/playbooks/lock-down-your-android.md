## What you'll be able to do afterward

- Your phone requires your fingerprint, face, or a strong PIN for anything that matters.
- A snatched phone locks itself and can't quietly disappear from your account.
- The advertising ID that follows you between apps is gone.
- If the phone vanishes, you can find, lock, or erase it from any browser.

## The plays

<ol class="plays">
<li>
<strong>Set a strong screen lock</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Security &amp; privacy → Device unlock → Screen lock → PIN (6+ digits)</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: made the lock — the key to everything else on this list — an actual barrier. Add fingerprint or face unlock on the same screen for convenience on top, not instead.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Turn on theft protection</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Security &amp; privacy → Device unlock → Theft protection → turn on Theft Detection Lock and Offline Device Lock</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: a phone snatched from your hand locks itself when it detects the grab motion, and going offline no longer keeps it usable.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Hide sensitive notifications on the lock screen</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Notifications → Notifications on lock screen → Hide silent conversations and notifications (or "Don't show notifications")</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: your messages and login codes no longer display to whoever is holding the phone.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Shorten the screen timeout</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Display → Screen timeout → 1 minute</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: a phone left on a table locks itself before it wanders off unlocked.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Make sure Find My Device is on</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Security &amp; privacy → Find My Device → on (check that "Store recent location" is allowed)</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: a lost phone is findable, lockable, and erasable from android.com/find — from any browser, any device.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Delete your advertising ID</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Privacy → Ads → Delete advertising ID</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: removed the serial number the ad industry uses to stitch your activity together across apps. Apps can still ask — they just get nothing stable back.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Audit the big three permissions</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Security &amp; privacy → Privacy controls → Permission manager → Location / Microphone / Camera — downgrade anything that doesn't obviously need it</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: cut standing access to your location, mic, and camera down to apps with a real reason. "Only while in use" beats "All the time," and "Don't allow" beats both.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Confirm Play Protect is scanning</strong>
<span class="path">Play Store → your profile icon → Play Protect → Scan — and check scanning is on under the gear icon</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: kept Android's built-in malware scanning active, which catches most of the junk that impersonates real apps.</p>
</li>
</ol>

## You're now covered against…

- **A stolen or lost phone** — grab-detection locks it, Find My Device finds it, and nothing readable sits on the lock screen.
- **The easy tracking** — no advertising ID to follow you, permissions trimmed to apps that earn them.
- **Impersonator apps** — Play Protect stays on watch.

Fifteen minutes, nothing to install, nothing to pay for. Menu names shift a
little between Android brands — if a path doesn't match, searching the
Settings app for the bolded words gets you there.
