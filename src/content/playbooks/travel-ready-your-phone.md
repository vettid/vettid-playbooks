## What you'll be able to do afterward

- A lost or stolen phone abroad is an inconvenience, not a catastrophe.
- You can find, lock, or erase it from any borrowed browser, and log in to do it without the phone in your hand.
- The stuff that matters exists somewhere besides your pocket.
- Your SIM can't be popped out and used by someone else.

## The plays

<ol class="plays">
<li>
<strong>Update everything before you leave</strong>
<span class="path">Where: system update in settings, plus app updates in the app store — the night before, on home Wi-Fi</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: patched the known holes before you carry the phone through crowds, shared networks, and borrowed chargers. Updating from a hotel network is the worse version of this play.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Run a full backup</strong>
<span class="path">Where: your phone's cloud backup settings — trigger a manual backup on Wi-Fi and confirm it completed</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: made the phone replaceable. If it's gone tomorrow, the photos, chats, and settings aren't.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Set a SIM PIN</strong>
<span class="path">Where: SIM security in your phone's cellular settings — use your carrier's PIN, not a guessable one</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: made the SIM useless in anyone else's phone. Without this, a thief can pop the card out and receive your calls and texts — including login codes — on their own device.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Test Find My from another device</strong>
<span class="path">Where: your platform's find-my portal, from a laptop or a family member's phone — locate your phone once, before the trip</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: proved the recovery path works while it doesn't matter. The middle of a bad day abroad is the wrong time to discover the toggle was off.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Make sure you can log in without the phone</strong>
<span class="path">Where: your account's recovery options — save or print backup codes; know which second factor works when the phone is the thing that's missing</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: broke the circular trap — "to lock the lost phone, log in; to log in, use the code sent to the lost phone." Backup codes in your bag (separate from the phone) cut the loop.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Download the offline versions</strong>
<span class="path">Where: offline maps for your destination, tickets and reservations saved to the device (and one printed copy if it's critical)</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: removed the pressure to join whatever open network is nearest the moment you land — the exact moment you're most likely to accept a bad one.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Decide what doesn't travel</strong>
<span class="path">Before you pack: remove apps you won't need that hold sensitive data — you can reinstall them at home in a minute</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: shrank what a lost, stolen, or inspected phone can give up. The easiest data to protect on the road is the data that stayed home.</p>
</li>
</ol>

## You're now covered against…

- **Loss and theft abroad** — findable, lockable, erasable, and backed up; the SIM is a brick outside your phone.
- **The lost-phone login trap** — backup codes mean losing the phone doesn't mean losing the accounts.
- **Desperation Wi-Fi** — offline maps and tickets mean you join networks by choice, not necessity.

Twenty minutes the night before beats any amount of scrambling from a
police-station lobby. Safe travels.
