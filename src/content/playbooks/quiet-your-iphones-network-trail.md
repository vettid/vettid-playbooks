## What you'll be able to do afterward

- Networks you join can't recognize the phone from last time.
- Your mail stops reporting when and where you read it.
- Sign-ups stop costing your real email address.

The universal network playbook covered the habits. These are the iOS
switches that go further.

## The plays

<ol class="plays">
<li>
<strong>Rotate your Wi-Fi address</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Wi-Fi → tap the ⓘ on a network → Private Wi-Fi Address → Rotating</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: made the phone introduce itself to this network with a changing hardware address, so "the device that was here Tuesday" stops being trackable across visits. Set it per network you care about — cafés and airports first.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Limit IP address tracking</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Wi-Fi → ⓘ → Limit IP Address Tracking (and the same toggle under Cellular → Cellular Data Options)</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: routed traffic to known trackers through a relay so they don't collect your IP — the address that links your activity across every site you visit from the same network.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Turn on Private Relay if you pay for iCloud+</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Private Relay</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: split your Safari browsing so no single party sees both who you are and where you're going — Apple sees your IP but not your sites, the second relay sees sites but not you. Honest framing: it's Safari-only and it's trusting Apple's design instead of a VPN company's promise. For many people that's a fine trade; know it's the trade.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Turn on Mail Privacy Protection</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Apps → Mail → Privacy Protection → Protect Mail Activity</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: stopped marketing emails from reporting back the moment you opened them and the IP you opened them from. The tracking pixel loads into a void.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Use Hide My Email for sign-ups</strong>
<span class="path">When: any sign-up that doesn't need your real identity — choose "Hide My Email" in the sign-up sheet (iCloud+ feature)</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: gave each service its own forwarding address. When one starts spamming or shows up in a breach, you know exactly who leaked, and you turn off that one address instead of abandoning your inbox.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Stop asking to join everything</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Wi-Fi → Ask to Join Networks → Off, and Auto-Join Hotspot → Never</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: stopped the phone from constantly probing for and offering to join networks around you — a quieter phone is a phone that's harder to inventory.</p>
</li>
</ol>

## You're now covered against…

- **Cross-visit recognition** — rotating addresses mean the café's network meets a stranger every time.
- **Email open-tracking** — marketing pixels report nothing useful.
- **Sign-up address harvesting** — every service gets a burner that answers to you.

Fifteen minutes of switches. None of them break normal use — the point of
these particular six is that you set them and forget they're on.
