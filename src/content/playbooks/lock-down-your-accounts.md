## What you'll be able to do afterward

- A leaked password from some site you forgot about can't unlock anything else you own.
- Your email — the master key to every reset — is the hardest account you have.
- A thief with your password still can't get in.
- If someone does try, you'll see it and know exactly what to do.

## The plays

<ol class="plays">
<li>
<strong>Lock down your email first</strong>
<span class="path">Where: your email provider's security settings — strong unique password, two-factor on</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: email is the skeleton key. Nearly every "forgot password" flow runs through it, so securing it first secures the reset path for everything else.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Turn on two-factor authentication everywhere it matters</strong>
<span class="path">Where: security settings of banking, email, social, and shopping accounts</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: a stolen password stops being enough to get in. Use an authenticator app over text codes where you can — phone numbers can be hijacked at the carrier.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>End password reuse with a manager</strong>
<span class="path">Where: your browser or phone's built-in password manager, or one you choose</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: reuse is how one leaked site becomes every site. A manager makes unique passwords the lazy path instead of the diligent one. (If every device you own is Apple or every device is Google, passkeys are a nice upgrade on top for your biggest accounts — otherwise skip them guilt-free: they sync poorly across ecosystems, and this play plus two-factor is a complete answer.)</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Point account recovery at yourself</strong>
<span class="path">Where: each major account's recovery email and recovery phone settings</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: stale recovery details are an unlocked side door. An old phone number can be reassigned to a stranger — who can then "recover" your account with it.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Review active sessions and sign out strangers</strong>
<span class="path">Where: security settings &rarr; "your devices" or "where you're signed in"</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: the device list is your account's guest log. Anything you don't recognize gets signed out — and the password changed while it's locked out.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Know the takeover drill</strong>
<span class="path">When: password-reset emails you didn't request, or login alerts from places you've never been</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: gave you the response before you need it — change the password from a device you trust, sign out every session, re-check recovery settings. Speed beats diagnosis.</p>
</li>
</ol>

## You're now covered against…

- **Credential stuffing** — the leaked-password-from-one-site trick dies when no password opens two doors.
- **Password theft** — two-factor means the password alone is a souvenir, not a key.
- **Recovery hijacks** — resets and recovery codes only go to places you still control.
- **Slow-motion takeovers** — you'll spot the warning signs and already know the drill.

Twenty minutes across your handful of accounts that actually matter.
None of it needs to be repeated tomorrow — this one compounds.
