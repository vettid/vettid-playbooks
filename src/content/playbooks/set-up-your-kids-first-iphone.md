## What you'll be able to do afterward

- Your kid has their own real account — not a login borrowed from yours — with protections that fit their age.
- Purchases, downloads, and screen time run through agreements, not arguments.
- The protection is something you did *with* them, not *to* them — which is the version that survives adolescence.

One principle before the plays: this setup is guardrails, not surveillance.
The goal is a kid who gradually earns wider access and knows how the
controls work — not one who gets good at evading controls they never agreed
to.

## The plays

<ol class="plays">
<li>
<strong>Create their own child account</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → [your name] → Family Sharing → Add Member → Create Child Account</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: gave them a real Apple account with age-appropriate defaults, connected to your family instead of a copy of your login. Their age on this account drives every protection that follows — set it truthfully.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Turn on Ask to Buy</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Family Sharing → [child's name] → Ask to Buy</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: routed every purchase and free download through an approval request on your phone. Not because apps are evil — because the approval moment is where you find out what they're into, together.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Set up Screen Time on their device</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Screen Time → set up as a parent, with a Screen Time passcode that isn't your phone passcode</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: created the enforcement layer the next plays configure — with a passcode they don't know, guarding the settings rather than the kid.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Set Downtime and App Limits together</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Screen Time → Downtime (e.g. bedtime to morning), and App Limits for the categories that eat evenings</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: put the day's shape into the phone — decided with them at the kitchen table, not announced by the device at 9 p.m. Phones make bad surprise-bearers and fine referees.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Set content restrictions to their age</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Screen Time → Content &amp; Privacy Restrictions → App Store, Media, &amp; Web content — age-appropriate ratings and "Limit Adult Websites"</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: matched what the phone will serve to who's holding it. The web filter is a coarse tool, not a guarantee — which is why it's one play here and not the whole playbook.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Turn on Communication Safety</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Screen Time → Communication Safety → Check for Sensitive Photos</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: made the phone itself intervene — on the device, nothing reported to Apple or to you — when nudity shows up in what they're sent or about to send, with age-appropriate guidance in the moment. It's a seatbelt, and it respects their privacy while being one.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Share locations as a family</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Family Sharing → Location Sharing — and say out loud that it goes both ways</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: made "where are you" a solved problem — and made it mutual, which is the difference between a family map and a tracking device. Kids accept guardrails they'd sabotage if they were one-way.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Lock the settings, not the conversation</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Screen Time → Content &amp; Privacy Restrictions → don't allow account changes and passcode changes; then agree on the review date — e.g. every birthday, limits loosen</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: stopped the protections from being quietly undone — and gave them the honest answer to "until when?" Guardrails with a published expansion schedule get respected; permanent ones get engineered around.</p>
</li>
</ol>

## You're now covered against…

- **The app store firehose** — everything installs through an approval you'll actually see.
- **The 1 a.m. phone** — downtime is policy, agreed in daylight.
- **The worst moments** — sensitive-photo intervention happens on-device, before the send.
- **The arms race** — mutual location, published rules, and a loosening schedule mean the phone isn't the enemy.

Thirty minutes of setup and one honest conversation. The conversation is
the security control — the settings just back it up.
