## Read this first

Same ground rules as the universal monitoring playbook, which is worth
reading first: if the person who might be monitoring you could harm you,
your safety comes before your privacy — consider reading from a device they
can't access, and know that **removing access is usually visible** to the
person who had it. Each play notes what they might notice. Advocates at a
domestic violence hotline and the guides at
<a href="https://www.techsafety.org">techsafety.org</a> can help you plan.

On iPhone specifically, most monitoring isn't hidden software — it's
legitimate sharing features quietly turned on: a shared account, location
sharing, a configuration profile. That's what these checks cover.

## The plays

<ol class="plays">
<li>
<strong>Run Safety Check in review mode</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Privacy &amp; Security → Safety Check → Manage Sharing &amp; Access</span>
<p class="did">What this does: walks you through everything your iPhone shares and who can access it — Apple built this screen for exactly this situation. Use "Manage" to review first; the "Emergency Reset" button on the same screen cuts everything at once, which is powerful and <em>immediately</em> visible. Visibility: reviewing is quiet; changing anything is not.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Check who sees your location</strong>
<span class="path">Find My app → People tab; and Settings → Privacy &amp; Security → Location Services → Share My Location</span>
<p class="did">What this does: lists every person receiving your location and which device broadcasts it. Note what you find before changing it. Visibility: stopping a share notifies the pattern — the person's map goes quiet.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Review the devices on your Apple account</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → [your name] → scroll to the device list at the bottom</span>
<p class="did">What this does: shows every device signed into your account — each one can receive your messages, photos, and backups. A device you don't recognize (or an old one in their possession) is monitoring with no software at all. Visibility: removing a device signs it out immediately and noticeably.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Check for configuration and management profiles</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → General → VPN &amp; Device Management</span>
<p class="did">What this does: surfaces installed profiles — the mechanism workplaces use to manage phones, and the mechanism monitoring products borrow. On a personal phone you set up yourself, this screen should be empty or absent. Document anything here before removing it. Visibility: removal breaks the profile's function at once.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Read the App Privacy Report</strong>
<span class="path">Settings → Privacy &amp; Security → App Privacy Report</span>
<p class="did">What this does: shows which apps have been using location, mic, and camera, and what they contact. An app you don't recognize with frequent sensor access is a candidate — note it, look it up from a safe device, don't delete it yet. Visibility: reading the report changes nothing.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>When you're ready: reset access on your schedule</strong>
<span class="path">How: per the universal playbook's plan-first play — pair Safety Check's Emergency Reset (or targeted changes) with a new passcode (Settings → Face ID &amp; Passcode) and a new Apple account password, ideally in one sitting</span>
<p class="did">What this does: makes the cut once, deliberately, everywhere at the same time — instead of a drip of visible changes. A passcode they know is monitoring too; it changes in the same sitting. Visibility: total, by design. This is the step to plan around, with an advocate if there's any safety concern.</p>
</li>
</ol>

## You're now covered against…

- **Sharing-feature monitoring** — every share, session, and profile reviewed by name.
- **Account-level access** — no unrecognized device rides your Apple account.
- **A rushed removal** — you know what's on, who'd notice a change, and the reset happens when you decide.

The universal monitoring playbook covers documentation, safety planning,
and the full clean-slate option — this one is the iPhone-specific layer,
not a replacement for it.
