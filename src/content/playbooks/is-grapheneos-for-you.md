## What you'll be able to do afterward

- You'll know whether GrapheneOS fits your phone, your apps, and your patience — before you commit to anything.
- The honest tradeoffs are on the table, not discovered three days in.
- If the answer is yes, the next playbook sets it up. If it's no, that's a fine answer too.

GrapheneOS is a security- and privacy-hardened Android that runs without
Google services by default. It's the strongest privacy posture a normal
person can carry in a pocket — and it asks some things of you in return.
These plays are decision checks, not settings.

## The plays

<ol class="plays">
<li>
<strong>Check the hardware requirement</strong>
<span class="path">Check: GrapheneOS supports Google Pixel phones (recent generations) — nothing else</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: settled the first gate. If you don't have a supported Pixel and won't buy one, the answer is no for now, and you just saved yourself the rest of the list.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>List the apps you can't live without</strong>
<span class="path">Write down: banking apps, work apps, payment/transit apps — the ones a phone is broken without</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: named the real compatibility question. Most apps run fine (including via sandboxed Google Play), but some banking and payment apps refuse hardened systems, and tap-to-pay with Google Wallet does not work. Check your critical few against the GrapheneOS community's app-compatibility reports before deciding.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Decide how Google-dependent you are</strong>
<span class="path">Consider: photos in Google Photos, backup habits, "find my" reliance, watch/earbuds pairing</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: sized the migration, not just the installation. On GrapheneOS, Google services are optional guests in a sandbox rather than the landlord — most things work, but nothing is seamless by default, and seamless was the product.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Check your patience budget honestly</strong>
<span class="path">Ask: am I willing to spend an evening installing, and occasional minutes troubleshooting what "just worked" before?</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: priced the ongoing cost. GrapheneOS is polished and boring in daily use, but you become your own IT department for the edge cases. Some people find that satisfying. Know which person you are.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Match it to your actual threat</strong>
<span class="path">Ask: is my concern the advertising economy, or a targeted person/organization?</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: calibrated expectations. For shrinking the surveillance economy's view of you, the earlier playbooks on a normal phone get you most of the way. GrapheneOS is for wanting the ceiling, not the floor — structurally less data leaving the device, and hardening against sophisticated attacks.</p>
</li>
<li>
<strong>Know the exit before the entrance</strong>
<span class="path">Check: the official web installer can also restore a Pixel to stock Android</span>
<p class="did">What this just did: removed the fear of commitment. Trying GrapheneOS is reversible in an afternoon — which makes "try it for two weeks" a legitimate decision, not a leap.</p>
</li>
</ol>

## You're now covered against…

- **The evangelist's version** — you've seen the tradeoffs (banking apps, tap-to-pay, DIY support) before switching, not after.
- **The wrong reasons** — you know what it's for and whether that's your actual problem.
- **Commitment fear** — the way back is as documented as the way in.

If it's a yes: the next playbook is your first day. If it's a no: the
quick-win and audit playbooks on your current phone are most of the value
with none of the move.
